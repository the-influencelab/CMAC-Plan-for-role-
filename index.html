The scenario

2am in the control centre

A flight is cancelled. Two hundred passengers need hotel rooms and coaches within the hour. The coordinator taking the call joined six weeks ago, the client is on the other line, and the duty manager is handling three other disruptions.

What happens next depends on how well that coordinator was onboarded, how clearly they can think under pressure, and whether their manager has been developed to lead them through it. That is what People Development at CMAC is for.

How I design learning that people use at work

These principles come from adult learning theory, neuroscience and my book, Modern Learning In A Digital World. They are what I would build the CMAC function on.

Data firstStart with data and observation, not a training request. The starting point decides the success.
Learn by doingKolb's cycle: experience, reflect, make sense of it, try it again on the real job.
Real scenariosPractise on CMAC's own disruptions, so the brain recognises the moment when it comes.

Ethical AI

I advocate the ethical use of AI. As it becomes easier to reach for, I encourage thinking space and intuition first, with AI used at the right time and in the right place. That protects people's cognitive autonomy, rather than defaulting to AI first every time.

Self regulation under pressure

Clear thinking is a skill, and it can be developed

Disruption work keeps people at the edge of their Window of Tolerance. When they move outside it, judgement, tone and accuracy drop. My Self Regulation framework gives people a shared language and a practical way back, always tied to a behaviour and an outcome.

Above the windowRushing, snapping, missing details on a busy shift.
Inside the windowCalm, accurate, clear with clients and passengers.
Below the windowSwitching off, going quiet, waiting for someone else to act.

The Navigator

Momentum and quick decisions.

Under pressure

Rebooks fast and skips the final check.

The outcome we want

Pause, confirm, then act. Fewer errors and repeat calls.

The Anchor

Steady and reliable.

Under pressure

Holds back a problem rather than raising it.

The outcome we want

Speaks up early, so issues are escalated in time.

The Architect

Clarity and structure.

Under pressure

Waits for more information before deciding.

The outcome we want

Decides with enough, not everything. Faster response times.

The Connector

Warmth with people.

Under pressure

Absorbs every passenger's distress.

The outcome we want

Warm with boundaries. Better wellbeing and steady service.

Where self regulation sits

Self regulation is not a standalone course. It is the thread running through every part of the function, so the same language is used from day one to leadership.

1

Onboarding

New starters take the diagnostic in week one and learn their way back into the window before their first live disruption.

2

Scenario practice

Every disruption scenario includes a pressure moment, so people practise staying clear, not just following the process.

3

Managers first

Managers learn to read their team's archetypes and lead them back into the window during peaks and night shifts.

4

Coaching and mentoring

Mentors use the shared language in one to ones and debriefs after difficult shifts.

5

Wellbeing and retention

Fewer people burning out or switching off, which supports attrition and quality measures.

My first 90 days

Listen, design, launch

  • Review, refine and scale.

Learning as a collective

Learning is not the sole responsibility of L&D

The strongest learning cultures are built together. I would build CMAC's as a collective, with the people who know the work best in the room.

  • Scenario based learning with mentors in the room. Experienced coordinators and managers join sessions, share how they handled real disruptions and coach in the moment.
  • Learning champions in every site and team. Champions keep learning alive between sessions, spot needs early and feed back what is working.
  • Cross pollination. Ideas and good practice shared across sites, countries, functions and acquired businesses, so no team solves the same problem alone.
  • Shared ownership. Leaders, mentors and champions co own outcomes with L&D, which is how learning sticks.
